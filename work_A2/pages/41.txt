The Rice Master of Data Science is a 31-semester-hour, non-thesis degree that takes three consecutive semesters to complete. The curriculum combines graduate-level statistics and computer science with advanced coursework in your specialization area. Your final semester culminates in the D2K (Data to Knowledge) capstone, working with a real industry partner on an actual business problem. Explore Tuition & Financial Resources Is the Rice Master of Data Science a thesis or non-thesis degree? The Rice MDS is a non-thesis master's degree. The D2K capstone project, in which you work with a real industry partner on an actual business problem, serves as your degree capstone experience. How long does the on-campus program take to complete? Three consecutive semesters (fall, spring, second fall) is the typical timeline. If pursuing an external internship, these are typically taken during the summer between semesters. What specializations are available? Rice offers five MDS specialization tracks: Artificial Intelligence, Business Analytics, Energy Transition and Sustainability, Image Processing (on-campus only) and Sports Analytics (on-campus only). What can I study on campus that isn't available online? The Image Processing, Energy Transition and Sustainability and Sports Analytics specializations are only offered in the on-campus program. How is the capstone structured? The D2K (Data to Knowledge) capstone takes place in your final semester. You'll work with a real industry partner on an actual business problem, building a portfolio piece that demonstrates impact. Is an internship required to graduate? No. The Professional Development requirement can be met through an internship, a relevant course or existing post-baccalaureate work experience. Do I have to choose between the online and on-campus MDS? Yes. Applicants apply to and are explicitly admitted into either the online or the on-campus program, not both. How does the application process work? Review the application requirements and deadlines for the Rice MDS through the Rice graduate application process.
