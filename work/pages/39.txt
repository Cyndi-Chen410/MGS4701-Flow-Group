How long is the program? The program is 15 months. For Fall 2026, classes begin September 24th and end December 10th of the following year. How large is the entering class? Our target class size for the entering Class of 2028 is 120 students. Where can I get an application? You may start a new application or return to an in-progress application here . Do I need to know programming languages to enter the program? While knowing programming languages such as Python and SQL has advantages for students, they are not required for admission. What if my submitted exam score is not reflecting on my application? e.g. GRE, GMAT, TOEFL, IELTS Please disregard the exam status on the application, as we have the data at the departmental level. Will the current updates on U.S. H-1B visa requirements affect me as a student or as an employee in the U.S. after I graduate? No. Switching from a student visa (F-1 or J-1) to an H-1B is not subject to the $100,000 fee, which is stated in some of the federal government’s H1B requirements . With respect to employment, our international graduating students qualify for OPT (Optional Practical Training, a status granted for graduates from STEM-designated programs) and after 36 months of employment can be sponsored by their employer or can apply for an H-1B through the lottery system and will NOT have to pay the $100,000 fee. Jump to Prospective Applicants International General Test Scores Work Experience Interview Financial Aid Career Services Prospective Applicants – General How is the MSBA program different than the MBA and MFE programs offered by UCLA Anderson? Learn More Business analytics is the application of statistical, computational, quantitative, econometric and management techniques to the information flows of an organization for the purpose of addressing business needs and improving decision making. ​ The MSBA program aims to give students the knowledge, training and skills required to fulfill a broad spectrum of business analytics roles. ​ The Anderson MBA programs offer a more extensive core curriculum and then allow students to choose a specialty in a particular area — strategy, marketing, finance, operations, entrepreneurship, high-technology management — with an aim to work in generally less technical or nontechnical management and leadership roles. MBA programs are longer in duration (two to three years) and require three to five years of work experience for admission.​ The MFE program differs from the MSBA program in that quantitatively focused MFE students take only courses directly related to modern finance theory and practice, such as stochastic calculus.​ Close How long is the program? See here What is the academic calendar schedule for the program? Learn More The MSBA Curriculum is held on-campus from Fall 2024 through Fall 2025, and it’s cohort based with two sections. The 15-month program begins in Fall 2026 quarter ( September 21, 2026 - December 11, 2026). Winter quarter (January 4, 2027 - March 19, 2027) continues through Spring quarter (March 24, 2027 - June 11, 2027) followed by a summer internship (June 2027- September 2027). The final segment of the program begins in the Fall 2027 quarter (September 20, 2027 - December 10, 2027). During the Fall 2025 quarter, continuing MSBA students must take campus-based classes. Upon successful completion of all requirements, students graduate from the program on December 10, 2027. Please note: Students cannot accelerate their studies and graduate early. This is a 15-month program, and the courses are only offered once a year. Close When is the deadline to apply? Close What is the expectation of effort at UCLA Anderson MSBA? Learn more High. The Master of Science in Business Analytics program is intersectional in its goals to educate with a cutting-edge research-based curriculum offered by our ladder faculty plus the applied training offered in our technical, industry, and career courses. In combination, UCLA Anderson MSBA courses prepare our graduates to answer questions of strategic importance, often early in their careers. Our recruiters have high expectations of you, and so do we. If you are a hiker who pays attention to the difficulty of your hike in advance, know that this one is hard. You will earn your accolades. Close Can I work while I am in the program? Learn More UCLA Anderson's MSBA Program is a full-time program. Part-time student status is not permitted. Students may work full-time (up to 40 hours per week) during their summer internship the quarter prior to graduation, and may work part-time (up to 20 hours per week) during Winter, Spring, and the final Fall quarter with program approval. Graduate students must maintain a GPA of 3.0 each quarter and cumulatively, prioritizing academics over employment. Close What type of degree does the admissions committee require for domestic and international applicants? Learn More At a minimum, students must satisfy the UCLA Graduate Division admissions requirements: The general requirement for admission for a U.S. student is a bachelor's degree from a regionally accredited institution, comparable in standard and content to a bachelor's degree from the University of California. A scholastic average of B (3.0 on a 4.0 scale) or better is required (or its equivalent if the letter grade system is not used) —for the last 60 semester units or last 90 quarter units of undergraduate study and in any post-baccalaureate study. Degrees which are not considered comparable include those granted on the basis of work completed at institutions which are not fully accredited and those granted on the basis of non-academic prior learning, test scores, or other than organized supervised coursework in academic subjects. An international student whose post-secondary education is completed outside of the U.S. is expected to hold a degree representing completion of at least four years of study with above average scholarship from a university or university-level institution. Close What is the ESLPE for Admitted Applicants? Learn More If you are an admitted applicant who has not met UCLA’s English language requirement , you must, upon arrival at UCLA, take the UCLA English as a Second Language Placement Examination (ESLPE). The ESLPE is an English diagnostic test. If you achieved a score of 100 or higher on the TOEFL iBT, or 7.5 or higher on the IELTS, the ESLPE requirement is waived ! If you scored below 100 on the TOEFL iBT, or below 7.5 on the IELTS, you must sit for the ESLPE upon arrival at UCLA. Depending on your results on the ESLPE, you may be required to complete English as a Second Language courses beginning in your first term at UCLA. If English courses are required, you should enroll in them right away, and you should also anticipate spending a longer period of time at the University. Admission is canceled for those who do not pass the ESLPE . Discovery, Netflix); and financial/fintech firms (e.g. PayPal, Intuit). Close Where can I find placement data from previous years? More See our Student Outcomes & Placement page . Close What is the salary of a new MSBA graduate? More For comparison, the average base salary for our Class of 2022 (December graduation) was 81% of the base salary for our MBA Class of 2023 (June graduation). Note that over half of MSBAs come to our program straight out of undergrad, while MBAs typically average 4-5 years of professional experience. See our Student Outcomes & Placement page . Close How hard will I have to work to get an analytics internship or full-time job? More Likely, very hard. Our program has an outstanding reputation and deep contacts in top organizations, and we prepare you for, and support you in, your search with best-in-class job search training, coaching, and tools. That said, you do have competition--now more than ever. It has also recently become more of a "hirer's market." The harder you work, especially in the areas of building and tapping your network and remaining constantly prepared for technical, analytics case, and behavioral interviews (for which we train you and provide ongoing support), the faster you are likely to land. Do not expect opportunities to be handed to you simply because you are in a top program. Close How does Career Services help students during more challenging economic climates? More In many ways. We teach job search strategies designed specifically for challenging times. We continue to source, and introduce students to, organizations that are hiring or are providing experience-building projects. We continue to introduce students to industry contacts who can ultimately support their career aspirations. And our outstanding curriculum prepares students for success over the long haul, regardless of any short-term bumps in the road. Close What can I do as a student to help Career Services help me in my internship/full time search process? More See our philosophy of collaboration on the Hollistic Career Services page. The partnership succeeds when the student embraces the career strategies we teach, takes advantage of the career and networking opportunities we provide, and proactively pursues a networking-based, efficient job search. Close Do you guarantee placement in an internship or a full-time job in the U.S. or elsewhere? More No, we do not. While we have a strong track record and work tremendously hard to help students land the roles they desire (see Hollistic Career Services ), we cannot promise that you will be placed or where a placement will occur (i.e. in the US). Close Which companies are included in the capstone, the "Applied Analytics Project." More The companies vary each year and typically include a mix of larger (e.g. Amazon, Honda, Microsoft) and smaller organizations. Students find that a company does not necessarily need to be large in order to provide an engaging project from which they learn a tremendous amount. About half our students choose to do a part-time internship instead of the capstone during their second fall quarter. Close What kind of roles do MSBA students usually land? More MSBA graduates land a wide range of roles across multiple industries, often based upon the level of technical ability and programming skills they bring to the program. Students with stronger technical backgrounds, if interested, are more likely to land roles like “data scientist” or “data engineer.” Students who are less experienced and/or interested in the programming aspects of analytics land roles like “data analyst,” “business analyst,” and “product manager.” See our Student Outcomes & Placement page .
